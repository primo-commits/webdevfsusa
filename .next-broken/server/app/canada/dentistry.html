The problems we hear most

Challenges Dentistry businesses face every day

Patients decline treatment plans because of cost

A comprehensive treatment plan at $5,000 is clinically necessary but financially out of reach for many patients without a payment option. The result is deferred treatment, ongoing pain, and lost revenue for the practice. The practices with financing options built in see significantly higher case acceptance.

Card processing fees on high-volume monthly billings add up

A busy practice processes $40,000/month in patient payments. At 2.5%, that's $1,000/month in processing fees, or $12,000/year. Over five years, $60,000 in fees on collections. For a practice trying to invest in new equipment or expand, that money matters.

Insurance reimbursements lag and create cash flow gaps

Insurance billing is a fact of dental practice. But the lag between completing work and receiving reimbursement creates real cash flow pressure. Growing practices need capital access to bridge the gap between service delivery and insurance payment.

Reactivation campaigns for lapsed patients rarely scale

Getting a lapsed patient to book again requires consistent outreach, which requires time and systems. Most practices rely on front desk staff to make calls, which competes with their primary job of checking patients in and out.
